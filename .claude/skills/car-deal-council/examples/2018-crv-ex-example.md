# Car Deal Council — Report: 2018 Honda CR-V EX

**Deal evaluated:** 2018 Honda CR-V EX, 71,000 miles, one owner (bought new Aug 30, 2018 at Gary Yeomans Honda, Daytona Beach FL), ~9,200 miles/year. One minor front-bumper accident on Carfax (Carfax adjustment: −$469). Marketed as ex-Navy/military owner.
Asking **$18,500**; out-the-door **$19,735** (6% tax $1,110 + $25 + $100 tag transfer).
Council convened July 3, 2026. Six agents, independent live web research; this is the good-friend synthesis.

---

## VERDICT: Fair deal — negotiate first. Good car, honest fee math, wrong price.

Here's the thing: this is a genuinely good car and a refreshingly honest quote structure — the fee math checks out to the dollar, which almost never happens. The problem is one number: **$18,500 is what clean-history 2018 CR-V EXs sell for, and this one has an accident on its record.** When the dealer acquired this car, the accident knocked an estimated $1,300–$2,200 off what they paid — that's how the wholesale market treats any reported accident. They're now asking you to accept $469 as the accident's full cost while they pocket the difference. The car is worth buying; the accident discount belongs to you, not them.

**Your numbers: open at $16,500 on the car. Settle at $17,300–$17,500 ($18,470–$18,680 out-the-door). Walk above $18,000 on the car (~$19,200 OTD)** — because at that point clean-history EXs and even leather-equipped EX-Ls exist at $18,988–$18,995.

## 1. The car itself — the best car the council has reviewed

The 2018 CR-V EX is close to the gold standard for used compact SUVs: RepairPal ranks it **#2 of 26** in its class at just **$407/year** average repair cost, real-world fuel economy is ~29 MPG, and owner forums are full of uneventful 100k–200k-mile examples. One owner, 9,200 miles/year, Florida (no rust) is a strong profile. Three things actually matter:

1. **The oil dilution issue — real, but this car is low-risk.** The 2017–2018 CR-V's 1.5-liter turbo engine had a known problem where gasoline seeps into the engine oil on cold starts. It's a cold-climate, short-trip problem — a lifelong Florida car is in the lowest-risk group in the country. Two catches: Florida cars never got Honda's software fix (Honda didn't offer it in warm states), and the extended warranty for it **expired August 30, 2024**. The check is simple: pull the dipstick cold — oil above the full mark or smelling like gasoline = problem. A $35 mail-in oil analysis (Blackstone) settles it definitively. Also demand oil-change records; this engine wants oil changes every ~5,000 miles, not 10,000.
2. **The AC system — a live warranty asset AND the accident's blind spot.** Honda extended the A/C compressor shaft-seal warranty on these to **10 years, unlimited miles — this car is covered until ~August 2028, and it transfers to you.** Real value on a Florida car. BUT: the AC condenser sits directly behind the front bumper that got hit, and crash damage is *not* covered — Honda will deny a claim if the failure traces to the collision. Test: idle in the sun, AC on max, 10 full minutes — it must blow painfully cold.
3. **The accident is the whole question.** Carfax's "minor" label comes from a data feed — nobody inspected the car. "Minor front bumper" can mean a parking tap or a hit that bent the radiator support behind a new bumper cover. The repair invoice and a pre-purchase inspection resolve this; a story does not.

Small stuff owners actually complain about: road noise (the #1 gripe), laggy infotainment, a battery-drain quirk (fixed by a software update, TSB 17-032), and a CVT "judder" that's usually a $400–800 fluid-and-reflash fix, not a failure. Also: **four recalls** on the 2018, including a 2023 fuel-pump recall expansion (23V-858) — any Honda dealer runs the VIN free.

## 2. The money

| Number | What it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| $18,500 asking | Top of *clean-history* retail. Clean 2018 EXs at ~71k retail for $17,500–$18,700; accident-adjusted fair retail is **$16,700–$17,600**. | $900–$1,800 over fair |
| $1,110 + $25 + $100 | 6% FL tax + Volusia County surtax (0.5% × first $5,000 = exactly $25) + plate transfer | Honest and exact ✓ |
| $19,735 OTD | Internally consistent — but overpriced by the same ~$1,000–$1,800 as the sticker | Negotiate the car, not the fees |

**Caveats on the "clean" fees:**
- The $100 tag assumes you're **transferring an existing Florida plate**. A new registration adds Florida's one-time **$225 initial registration fee** (+$28 plate) — ask which applies to you.
- The $25 surtax assumes you register in **Volusia County**; other counties differ slightly.
- No doc fee appears — unusual in Florida (median ~$899). If the seller is Gary Yeomans Honda, they advertise a $0 doc fee, which fits. But that same dealership has a BBB complaint on record for an **$899 "reconditioning fee"** appearing on a used car at signing. The quote is only real on a **signed buyer's order** stating "out the door, no additional fees."

**The $469 Carfax number is not a valuation.** Carfax never inspected this car; that figure is a listing adjustment. KBB pegs the average accident-history value loss at ~$1,700; diminished-value studies say 10–15% even after quality repair. The dealer's own appraisal software applied that bigger discount when they *bought* the car. The $469 works for whoever is selling — don't let it work against you.

## 4. Your negotiation script

- **Open:** "I'll do **$16,500 on the car** — that's clean-EX retail minus what the market actually discounts for a reported accident — pending the repair invoice and my mechanic's inspection."
- **When they point at the Carfax $469:** "That's a listing adjustment, not a valuation — Carfax never saw this car. KBB says accident history costs ~$1,700. And your appraisal software took more than $469 off when you bought it."
- **When they say Hondas are firm:** "Clean-history 2018 EXs are listed from $17,700 and EX-Ls at $18,988. Firm pricing works on cars without an asterisk."
- **Use the maintenance due:** CVT fluid is overdue at 71k, brake fluid due, rear pads likely worn (these eat rear pads early), tires may be sun-aged — $500–$1,500 of immediate catch-up. "Either do the CVT and brake fluid service before delivery or take it off the price."
- **Settle:** $17,300–$17,500 on the car. **Ceiling: $18,000. Walk trigger: any new fee at signing, refused inspection, or no repair documentation.**
- Ask one bonus question: "Will you sell it as **HondaTrue Used**?" (Honda's program for up-to-10-year-old cars: 100-day/5,000-mile warranty + roadside.) If it's a Honda store, that costs them little and is worth real money to you.

## 5. Before you sign — walk in with this

**Bring:** credit-union pre-approval, printed clean-history comps (Carfax/CarGurus 2018 EX listings), this checklist.

**Demand in writing:**
1. Signed itemized buyer's order: $XX,XXX + tax + surtax + tag, "no additional dealer/doc/recon/e-filing fees," and whether tag assumes plate transfer.
2. **The accident repair invoice** — bumper-cover-only, or did it touch the radiator support, condenser, headlamps, or Honda Sensing radar? Insurance claim or out-of-pocket? OEM or aftermarket parts?
3. Fresh Carfax AND AutoCheck (AutoCheck catches auction lanes Carfax misses). Compare accident date to trade-in date — a quick sale after the crash is a tell.
4. Service records, especially oil-change frequency (dilution history) and any AC service.
5. FTC Buyers Guide sticker: AS-IS or warranty? Verbal promises die when the as-is box is checked.
6. VIN run for recalls (fuel pump 23V-858, EPS, seat belt) — free at any Honda dealer.

**The inspection ($150–$300, your shop, non-negotiable):**
- Cold engine: dipstick level and smell (gasoline = dilution). Optionally a $35 oil analysis.
- Front-end forensics on a lift: radiator support (welds, kinks, non-factory bolts), panel gaps, headlight match, overspray, crash bar, condenser condition. Full module scan including airbag and Honda Sensing radar codes.
- AC: 10 minutes at idle, max cold, Florida sun — painfully cold or no deal.
- CVT hot and cold: shudder at light throttle 15–40 mph, whine, flare, delayed engagement = walk.
- Rear brake pads, tire date codes, battery test; trunk spare-well check for flood tells (coastal car).

**Walk away instantly if:** the repair reached the radiator support/airbags/structure, the AC isn't cold, gas-smelling overfull oil with no records, flood tells, they refuse the inspection, or a new fee materializes at signing.

## 📱 Dealership cheat sheet

**VERDICT:** Fair deal, wrong price. Good car IF the inspection clears the accident.
**NUMBERS:** Open **$16,500** → settle **$17,300–$17,500** on the car (**~$18,500–$18,700 OTD**). Ceiling $18,000. Their $18,500 = clean-car money for an accident car.

**ASK:**
1. "Show me the accident repair invoice — what exactly was replaced, and did it touch the radiator support or AC condenser?"
2. "Signed buyer's order at this OTD, marked 'no additional fees' — including whether the $100 tag assumes I transfer a Florida plate."
3. "Oil change records, and run the VIN for the fuel-pump recall and AC warranty-extension status."

**REFUSE:**
1. The $469 Carfax number as the accident's "cost" — the market discount is $1,700+.
2. Any fee, product, or "reconditioning" charge appearing at signing.
3. Financing without comparing to your credit-union pre-approval.

**WALK IF:** no repair documentation, no independent inspection allowed, AC not ice-cold, gas smell on the dipstick, or the OTD grows at signing.

**REMEMBER:** AC compressor seal is under Honda warranty until ~Aug 2028 and transfers to you — verify by VIN. That's yours; don't let them sell it back to you as a "warranty."
