# Car Deal Council — Example Report

**Deal evaluated:** 2020 Mitsubishi Outlander ES FWD, 3-row, 87,000 miles, visible wear.
Advertised $9,984 → financed out-the-door ~$11,000 → cash out-the-door $13,000.63.
Council convened July 2, 2026. Six agents (deep researcher, market analyst, sketchy dealer, customer, mechanic, skeptic) each did independent live web research; this is the good-friend synthesis.

---

## VERDICT: Bad deal at $13,000.63 cash — negotiate hard or walk

Here's the thing: the *car* is priced fairly, but the *deal* is not. The $9,984 sticker is real-market money for this car. The problem is the dealer added roughly $3,017 in "fees" on the cash deal when legitimate tax, title, and paperwork should run about $1,300–$1,800 in most states. About $1,200–$1,700 of that cash quote is pure dealer padding. And the reason cash costs $2,000 MORE than financing isn't a math error — the dealer makes $1,000–$2,500 in hidden profit when you take their loan (banks pay them a kickback for marking up your interest rate), so they jack up the cash price to replace that lost profit. The cash quote is the honest one: $13,000.63 is what they actually want to make on you.

**Fair number for this exact car: roughly $10,800–$11,200 cash out-the-door, all fees included** — and only after a clean history report and an independent mechanic checks the transmission.

---

## 1. The car itself

The 2020 Outlander is a middle-of-the-road car: cheap to run (~$519/year average repairs per RepairPal, better than average), comfortable, slow, and worth little at resale. Three things actually matter at 87,000 miles:

1. **The transmission (CVT).** This car uses a CVT — a belt-driven automatic transmission. It's the single thing that can total this car financially: replacement runs $4,500–$7,500, which is half the car's value. The good news from real owner data: this generation's CVT is much better than its reputation, and failures cluster in cars whose fluid was *never changed*. The fluid should be serviced every 30,000 miles — so this car should have had 2–3 services by now. **No CVT service records = assume the worst.** Failures typically show up at 100,000–125,000 miles — exactly the next few years of this car's life. There's an active class action about this transmission (shudder, surging, delayed acceleration).
2. **The touchscreen.** The #1 complaint on the 2020 specifically: the screen dies entirely and dealers only replace it — $3,200. Test every function before buying.
3. **The third row is nearly decorative.** Owner consensus: kids-only, emergency use. Don't pay a 7-seater premium for it.

Also: two recalls to check by VIN at nhtsa.gov — a 2nd-row seat belt recall (2020) and a **brand-new April 2026 recall** for liftgate gas springs that corrode and can let the liftgate fall on you. Both fixed free at any Mitsubishi dealer.

## 2. The money

| Number | What it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| $9,984 advertised | Real-market price. Dealer retail for this car (87k mi, worn) is $10,500–$12,500; KBB private-party is $9,885–$12,360. Dealer likely paid $6,500–$7,800 at auction. | Fair, not a steal |
| ~$11,000 financed OTD | Only ~$1,016 of fees — suspiciously *low*. In most states that can't even cover sales tax + title + doc fee. It's a teaser that either grows at the finance desk or is subsidized by loan kickbacks. | Bait |
| $13,000.63 cash OTD | $3,017 above sticker (~30% in "fees"). Legitimate tax/title/doc explains at most ~$1,800. The rest is add-on junk (doc fee padding, "reconditioning," VIN etch, nitrogen, protection packages). | $1,700–$2,300 over market |

**Why cash costs more than financing:** dealers average $850–$1,200+ per car in finance income by marking up your loan rate 1–2.5 points (the "finance reserve" kickback) plus selling warranties and add-ons into the payments. A cash buyer pays none of that, so the dealer moves the profit into the cash price. This is a documented industry pattern (NerdWallet, Center for Responsible Lending, Consumer Reports), and the FTC warned 97 dealer groups in March 2026 about exactly this — advertising a price that's only available if you finance.

**The counter-exploit:** take their cheaper financed deal, verify in the contract's Truth-in-Lending box that there's **no prepayment penalty**, then pay the loan off in month one. You get their subsidized price; they lose the kickback. Federal law bans prepayment penalties on auto loans over 60 months; shorter loans must disclose them.

## 3. How the dealer is playing this deal

| Tactic seen here | Counter |
|---|---|
| Bait price ($9,984) that only exists online | "I only discuss the full out-the-door price, itemized in writing." |
| Cash OTD higher than financed | "The cash price is higher because you lose the lender kickback. Itemize both quotes side by side or I walk." |
| Precise-cents quote ($13,000.63) as fake legitimacy | "That number is a fee stack. Break out every line: price, tax rate, title, doc, add-ons." |
| Vague warranty, never in writing | "Who administers it? Give me the contract to read overnight. What's excluded, what's the deductible, where can I use it?" |
| "Wear and tear" admitted but never itemized | "List every known defect in writing on the buyer's order." |
| Monthly-payment talk / 4-square worksheet | "No monthly payments. Total price, APR, term only." |
| "Today only" pressure | "If the price expires when I leave, it was never real." |
| Pre-loaded add-ons ("already installed") | "Per the FTC I can't be charged for add-ons I didn't agree to. Remove them all." |

**On the warranty questions specifically:** Mitsubishi's famous 10-year/100,000-mile powertrain warranty is **original-owner only** — as the second owner you get the balance of the 5-year/60,000-mile basic warranty, which is expired on both age and miles. Factory coverage on this car is **$0** unless it's sold as Mitsubishi Certified Pre-Owned (ask — it almost certainly isn't at this price/condition). So any "warranty" they mention is a third-party service contract, typically marked up 100–300%, often powertrain-internals-only with a $100–$200 per-visit deductible and must-service-here clauses. Unless the written contract explicitly names the CVT as covered, it's worthless on this car. And no — most of these can NOT be used "anywhere"; the contract dictates where.

## 4. Negotiation script

- **Open:** "I'll pay **$10,500 cash out-the-door**, every fee and tax included, pending a clean Carfax/AutoCheck and an independent inspection."
- **Settle at:** **$11,200 out-the-door**, absolute ceiling.
- When they say fees are fixed: "Tax, title, and registration are fixed. Doc fees, reconditioning, etch, and protection packages are your profit, not the state's. Take them off or take the same amount off the car price."
- When they push financing: "Fine — show me the buy rate versus my rate, and show me in the contract there's no prepayment penalty. I'm pre-approved at my credit union at X%." (Get pre-approved first; mid-2026 average used-car APR is ~11.4%, prime ~9%.)
- When they refuse to itemize: walk. Every dealer system prints an itemized buyer's order in 30 seconds.
- **Never reveal you're a cash buyer until the out-the-door price is locked in writing.**

## 5. Before you sign — non-negotiable checklist

1. **Carfax AND AutoCheck** before negotiating: accidents, title brand, rental/fleet history (base-trim FWD Outlanders are archetypal ex-rentals), number of owners, auction churn, and any CVT fluid service records.
2. **Independent pre-purchase inspection** ($130–$250, your shop, not theirs): scan for transmission trouble codes, check CVT fluid condition, lift inspection. Dealer refuses = walk, full stop.
3. **The CVT road test** (20+ minutes, cold and hot): floor it up an on-ramp — engine revving high while the car builds speed slowly, or a rumble-strip shudder at steady 40–45 mph, means walk away at any price. Listen for whine that rises with road speed.
4. Test **every touchscreen function** ($3,200 if it dies).
5. Check tire date codes match and aren't 5+ years old; cold-start the engine yourself.
6. VIN through nhtsa.gov/recalls — seat belt (20V-280) and April 2026 liftgate springs (26V-252) closed or scheduled in writing.
7. Itemized OTD for BOTH cash and financed, warranty contract (not the pitch), FTC Buyers Guide window sticker (is it AS-IS?), title in hand, deposit terms in writing.

Budget reality even if all checks pass: $1,200–$2,500 of catch-up maintenance in year one (CVT fluid service ~$250, spark plugs ~$250, coolant, likely brakes/tires) — use it as negotiating ammunition.

## 6. If you walk — what the same money buys (July 2026 market)

- **2017–2019 Dodge Grand Caravan** (~$9k–$13k): a real 7-seater, conventional transmission, same money. Thirstier and blander.
- **2017–2018 Kia Sorento V6** (~$9.5k–$13k): nicer, genuinely midsize 3-row, regular automatic. Verify engine recall work done.
- **2017–2019 Hyundai Santa Fe XL** (from ~$13k): V6, real third row — top of budget.
- **2013–2015 Toyota Highlander** (~$13k, 110–130k mi): older and higher-mileage, but a 125k Highlander V6 has better odds of reaching 200k than an 87k Outlander CVT — and holds value.
- **2013–2014 Honda Pilot** (~$9k–$11.7k): cheaper than this deal, 8 real seats, stout V6.

At $13,000.63 you'd be paying Highlander/Sorento money for the weakest 3-row in the class with zero warranty.

## 📱 Dealership cheat sheet

**VERDICT:** Bad at $13,000.63. Target **$11,200 cash OTD max** (open at $10,500). Or take the ~$11,000 financed deal, confirm NO prepayment penalty in the contract, pay it off month one.

**ASK:**
1. "Itemized out-the-door sheet for cash AND financed, side by side, in writing."
2. "CVT fluid service records — show me every one. And is this car Mitsubishi Certified Pre-Owned?"
3. "The actual warranty contract: who backs it, does it name the CVT, what's the deductible, where can I use it?"

**REFUSE:**
1. Any add-on you didn't ask for (etch, nitrogen, protection, "recon fee") — FTC says they can't charge you for these.
2. Any monthly-payment conversation before the total price is locked.
3. Signing anything without an independent inspection.

**WALK AWAY IF:** they won't itemize, they won't allow your mechanic to inspect it, the transmission shudders/whines/revs-without-going on the test drive, there are zero CVT service records, or the title/history shows accidents or a brand.
